Research Associate · Harvard SEAS

Sajani Vithana

I work on the information-theoretic foundations of reliable and trustworthy AI.

AI models are noisy, stochastic components. Engineers have spent decades making communication networks, storage and navigation dependable despite noise and imperfect parts. I bring those tools (information theory, coding theory, statistics and optimization) to AI, to find what is fundamentally achievable and design algorithms that reach it.

The approach

Rigor over heuristics.

The usual way to make AI more reliable is to scale up data and models and check the results empirically. That approach gives no provable guarantees. Many reliable and trustworthy-AI problems are information-theoretic and statistical problems at the core. I analyze what is mathematically possible and what is impossible, and let those limits guide algorithm design.

Detecting a watermark is a hypothesis test
Getting reliable answers from unreliable models is a sequential decision problem
Privacy is a bound on information leakage
Evaluating a model is a search for failures with few, noisy tests

Research

Five connected thrusts.

Each thrust pairs a fundamental question with algorithms that come provably close to the limits it reveals.

01

AI Reliability

How can we build reliable AI systems from unreliable models, at the lowest possible cost?

In the 1950s, von Neumann, and Moore and Shannon, showed that reliable machines can be built from unreliable components by arranging them with redundancy. We apply the same idea to LLMs. Each model, together with its prompt, is a noisy measurement instrument. Reliable prediction then becomes the design of a circuit of instruments: which to query, in what order, and when to stop.

LMCircuit

New

Routing, cascading and majority voting are all special cases of circuits of LLM classifiers. We formulate circuit design as a Markov decision process, so the optimal circuit is the solution of a dynamic program, and we derive the best accuracy achievable under a given cost budget. LMCircuit learns this circuit from data and improves the cost–accuracy frontier over state-of-the-art routing and cascading methods.

Stochastic errors: the same model gives different answers to the same prompt. Repeated or correlated queries reduce them.
Systematic errors: the model is consistently wrong on a prompt. Only decorrelated instruments (different prompts or different models) reduce them.
  • Circuits of LLM Classifiers
02

AI Accountability

Can we trace AI-generated content back to its source without degrading it, and stop agents from hiding messages in it?

We treat LLM watermarking as a hidden communication channel between the model and a verifier, with the secret key acting as shared randomness. Seen this way, information theory and coding theory tell us how much can be embedded in generated text without changing its quality. The same view also exposes a new threat: AI agents talking to each other covertly.

SimplexWater & HeavyWater

NeurIPS 2025

Single-bit watermarks that provably preserve text quality and achieve state-of-the-art detection. An optimal-transport coupling between each next-token distribution and the secret key keeps the output distribution unchanged, so only the key holder can detect the watermark. SimplexWater comes from the binary simplex code and is provably optimal within its class. HeavyWater has the best detection in practice.

Its optimal-transport approach is used in OpenAI's deployed watermark.

  • HeavyWater and SimplexWater: Watermarking Low-Entropy Text Distributions

ArcMark

NeurIPS 2026

Multi-bit watermarking embeds a full message, such as a user ID, the model version, or even the prompt. We cast distortion-free multi-bit watermarking as channel coding with side information and derive its capacity, the fundamental limit on how much can be embedded. ArcMark approaches this limit in some cases and reliably embeds several bytes in a few hundred tokens.

  • ArcMark: Distortion-Free Multi-Byte LLM Watermark via Optimal Transport

Covert agentic communication

New

Two agents that share a key can exploit the same mechanism to talk secretly, in text that is provably indistinguishable from ordinary output. The sender sees every token the receiver sees, so this is a channel with noiseless feedback. Our scheme, Burnashev Adaptive Posterior Matching (BAM), chooses each embedded symbol from the receiver's current belief and stops once the receiver is confident.

  • Feedback Coding Enables Inference-Time Covert Agentic Communication
  • Interactive demo: can AI agents secretly play chess?
03

Data Privacy

How can AI learn from sensitive data and answer sensitive queries without leaking them?

Privacy matters at two stages. Training: private synthetic data generation and private federated learning. Inference: private retrieval-augmented generation, keeping user queries hidden from external databases.

Training

PubMix

ICML 2026

Standard DP mechanisms add the same Gaussian or Laplace noise no matter what is already public. PubMix is a domain-aware mechanism: public data from the same domain shows which directions to perturb in, and the optimal noise turns out to be a floor-raised version of the public distribution. PubMix also uses the randomness of sampling the synthetic data as a source of privacy, which significantly improves the privacy-utility trade-off.

  • Optimal Domain-Aware Privacy Mechanisms for Synthetic Data Generation

CorDP-DME

SaTML 2025 · ISIT 2025

Differentially private mean estimation for federated learning. Local DP is lightweight but inaccurate, with MSE \(O(1/n)\). Central DP reaches \(O(1/n^2)\) but needs a trusted server. Using optimally correlated noise, CorDP-DME needs no trusted party, always beats LDP, costs far less than secure aggregation, and keeps its guarantees under dropouts and collusion.

  • Correlated Privacy Mechanisms for Differentially Private Distributed Mean Estimation
  • DP Distributed Mean Estimation with Constrained User Correlations

Private Read-Update-Write

Best Paper

An information-theoretic framework for reading and writing parts of a model with perfect privacy and accuracy. It hides both which parameters a user touches and the update values, as needed in federated submodel learning and top-\(r\) sparsification. The codes are built from Lagrange polynomials and Cauchy–Vandermonde matrices, and their communication cost is asymptotically only 2× that of non-private FL.

  • PRUW in Federated Submodel Learning (IEEE Trans. IT)
  • PRUW with Controllable Information Leakage (IEEE Trans. IT)
  • Private FSL with Storage Constrained Databases (IEEE Trans. IT)

Inference

Private nearest-neighbor search for RAG

ISIT 2024

RAG sends a user's query embedding to an external vector database, which can leak sensitive information. Using hierarchical Reed–Solomon codes over \(N\) non-colluding databases, our scheme finds the nearest neighbor with perfect information-theoretic query privacy and no loss in accuracy. Its communication cost is \(O(N^2 M^{1/(N-1)})\), compared with \(O(\sqrt{M}\log M)\) for competing protocols.

  • Private Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search for Vector Database Querying

Semantic private information retrieval

IEEE Trans. IT

Classical private information retrieval assumes all files have the same length and are equally popular. Semantic PIR drops those assumptions. We derived its capacity, including for MDS-coded and colluding databases, and showed that it always outperforms classical PIR.

  • Semantic Private Information Retrieval
  • PIR and Its Applications: An Introduction, Open Problems, Future Directions
04

AI Evaluation Ongoing

How do we find where a model fails when we don't know where to look?

Benchmarks and leaderboards show how a model does where we already chose to look. They miss silent regressions after updates, weaknesses hidden by averages, and behaviors with no ground truth, such as tone, refusal or advice. We treat evaluation as an adaptive search over descriptors. Descriptors are natural-language classes of prompts, organized in a tree that grows deeper where the evidence points and wider into regions no existing dataset covers.

1Representation

How should the space of prompts be organized so that it can be searched?

2Exploration

How do we reach regions no dataset covers, and know how much we've explored?

3Efficient measurement

On a limited budget, which classes do we test, and when do we split them?

4Validity

Do generated prompts actually show the behavior their descriptor names?

Tools: Bayesian hierarchical models · multi-armed bandits · active learning · MDP planning

05

Socially Responsible AI

How do we measure and fix representational harms across intersecting identities?

Generative models misrepresent social and historical reality when they miss the context of a query. Most fairness work looks at single attributes such as race or gender. Real identities are intersectional.

Multi-Group Proportional Representation

NeurIPS 2024 · CVPR 2025

MPR is a theoretically grounded metric for intersectional representation. It compares retrieved or generated samples with a proportionally representative population using maximum mean discrepancy over a rich class of statistics. Building on MPR, the MOPR retrieval algorithm improves both accuracy and representation, and MPR-based fine-tuning closes representation gaps in text-to-image diffusion models while preserving image quality.

  • Multi-Group Proportional Representation in Retrieval
  • Multi-Group Proportional Representations for Text-to-Image Models
